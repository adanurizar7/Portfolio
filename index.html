Fraud Detection Model

Wake Forest MSBA — Machine Learning, Team of 5 (Class Case)
0.881
AUC on unseen data

My role: Built a logistic regression model in Python to flag likely fraud when a bank account application is submitted, using 247,111 applications from a 43-table bank database. I combined my teammates' feature ideas into 55 candidate features and narrowed them to 20 with statistical tests and stepwise selection.

  • Reached an AUC of 0.881 on a month of data the model never saw, vs. 0.606 for the bank's current fraud score
  • Set the review cut-off by weighing the cost of missed fraud against the cost of reviewing good customers
  • The cut-off catches 64% of fraud while reviewing 11.5% of applications, lowering estimated monthly fraud costs from $1.24M to $0.67M

Manufacturing & logistics under margin pressure

Manufacturers are under constant pressure to squeeze cost-per-man-hour and throughput without added headcount — the exact problem I worked on daily at Shaw. Consulting and analytics teams that serve industrial clients need people who've seen a shift-level P&L conversation happen in real time, not just in a case study.

Analytics is moving from reporting to prescribing

Dashboards that just report what happened are table stakes now; the value is in tools and analyses that tell a supervisor or a client what to do next. That's the shift I made building the Shaw KPI system, and it's the same shift consulting/analyst work asks for at a larger scale.
